# Asset sources

Provenance for everything the game ships or builds at startup. All of it is
original work made for this project, except the bundled Noto fonts
(third party, SIL Open Font License) and the whistle recording the user
chose (third party, licence unknown, **not cleared for release**; see
"Third-party audio" below). No other image, model or sound files from
elsewhere are shipped, and no network service or asset store is used.

## Audio — original, generated

All 77 WAVs are generated by [`tools/gen_audio.py`](../tools/gen_audio.py)
(Python standard library only, fixed seed `1997`, deterministic; the twelve
whistles are processed from the third-party recording below): 16-bit
PCM, mono, 44.1 kHz. The llano's own things are placed (panned to their
direction, faded with distance and behind walls by `Tuning::heard`): the
machine loops, the frogs, the windmill, the dynamo, Tureco, teammates'
footsteps, marks, the altar, the key box, the beacon, the cattle, the
thunder (from the bolt) and an omen's clatter behind you. The whistle and
everything whose source is him stay unplaced (`src/audio.rs`).

Regenerate: `python3 tools/gen_audio.py` (writes into `assets/audio/`);
`--only-whistles` rebuilds just the whistle takes, and `--only-whistles
--synth --out DIR` builds the synthesized fallback whistles elsewhere, as if
the recording were absent. `python3 tools/whistle_lab.py` plays them as the
game mixes them; `python3 tools/whistle_lab.py measure --check` (add
`--synth` for the fallback) fails unless the three distances stay apart on
air, brightness, slap, ring and gaps, at the game's slowest and fastest
playback speed too, and unless all twelve files are equally loud.

The three whistle distances are loudness-matched, so the game's gains are
the only level difference between them; each distance is a signature
instead. Every whistle file's loudest second is brought to the same RMS
(0.3, about −10.5 dBFS) and a look-ahead limiter (10 ms, 0.12 s recovery)
holds the few peaks under 0.97; the limiter works hardest, up to 6 dB, on
one short piece of far takes 0 and 1 that surfaces between two gusts. The
recording was cleaned of its own air, so the breath and air are added back
in processing: a band of noise riding the tone (the tone times slowly
wandering noise) and hiss following its envelope. The synthesized fallback
has its own band of air (noise through a resonance that follows its pitch)
and goes through the same three distances.

The game plays the three at `src/tuning.rs` gains `gain_loud` 1.0,
`gain_mid` 0.3 and `gain_faint` 0.1 (0 / −10.5 / −20 dB), ducks the night
bed to 0.4 under any whistle and plays each phrase at a random speed of
0.92–1.06, all under the master slider alone (the curve in `src/mix.rs`:
the default 80% is 12 dB below full scale). To A/B them by ear as the game
mixes them: `python3 tools/whistle_lab.py ladder --all-takes`.

## Third-party audio — not cleared for release

Converted to 44.1 kHz mono with the video's hum and hiss removed (ffmpeg high-pass, low-pass, `afftdn`) |

The twelve `whistle_*` files are built from it by `tools/gen_audio.py`.
Before any public release, clear the rights with the recording's owner or
delete `audio/source/` and regenerate: without the file, `gen_audio.py`
falls back to its own synthesized whistle (the original-work default).

## Textures and material maps — original, generated at runtime

`src/world/texture.rs` builds every surface texture at startup from the seed
(tileable value noise, strokes, weave patterns, stencilled lettering), each
with a box-filtered mip chain: ground grass/soil, road earth, weathered and
dark wood, rusted corrugated zinc, ceiba bark, foliage, burlap, straw, dark
and pale cloth, clay, water, the note paper, the 1998 calendar page, the
"HATO LA CEIBA" sign, nine painted location signs (original 5×7 stencil
glyphs) and the moon halo. Tangent-space normal maps (derived from each
albedo's luminance or a height field) and glTF-layout roughness/metallic
maps cover ground, road, wood, zinc, bark, burlap, straw, cloth and water.
`src/world/mesh.rs` supplies the tangent basis those maps need.

## Meshes and characters — original, generated at runtime

collides (`src/world/`). Where a glTF model below exists, it replaces the
procedural stand-in once loaded (`src/world/models.rs`); the stand-in stays
if the model cannot load.

## glTF models — original, built by script in Blender

Every model is built from code (lofted tubes, ribbons and primitives; no
scans, downloads or AI meshes), with procedural Cycles materials baked to
plain textures from small tileable patterns made in numpy. Deterministic
(fixed seeds). Rebuild headless with Blender 5.2, e.g.
`PATH=/usr/bin:$PATH blender -b --factory-startup --python tools/models/truck.py`,
or run the same file through the Blender MCP. Binaries are kept in Git LFS.

## Text

The pages (`src/lore.rs`: notes, a letter, ledgers, a clipping, the back of
a photograph, almanac margins, a copla, a telegram, the parish register, a
watch log, a prayer card, a radio broadcast), HUD and menus are original
writing, Spanish first with English beneath. The legend itself (the son, the
deer's entrails, the father, the grandfather's curse, the whip, the ají, the
dog Tureco, the sack of bones, the inverted whistle) is Venezuelan folklore;
the Hacienda Santa Rosa, its people and every page's words are fiction
written for this game.
